Mission Reflection

Working through this Multi-Cloud Explorer mission gave me a much clearer picture of how AWS, Azure, and GCP actually differ in practice, not just in theory. Of the three, Google Cloud Platform impressed me the most, largely because of how tightly its AI/ML tooling and Kubernetes support are woven into the rest of the platform — it felt purpose-built for modern, data-heavy workloads rather than bolted on afterward.

At the same time, researching all three made the similarities between them just as clear as the differences. Every provider offers the same core building blocks — virtual machines, object storage, managed relational databases, identity and access management, and container orchestration — just under different names and with different levels of polish. That consistency made it much easier to compare them side by side.

What stood out most was how business requirements, not raw technical specs, should really drive platform selection. A cash-strapped startup, a Microsoft-centric university, an AI research lab, and a global e-commerce company all had very different "best" answers, even though the underlying services were often functionally similar. Cost structure, existing vendor relationships, and workload type mattered more than which provider was "objectively best."

Acting as a Cloud Solutions Consultant for the four CloudNova clients pushed me to think less like a student memorizing service names and more like someone who has to justify a recommendation with real tradeoffs in mind. It wasn't enough to know what a service did — I had to explain why it was the right fit for that specific client's constraints.

Finally, my GitHub Cloud Computing Portfolio is noticeably stronger after this mission. It now documents not just infrastructure basics from Lab 2, but a full multi-cloud comparison, client-facing recommendations, and hands-on Linux investigation — evidence I can point to of practical, provider-agnostic cloud reasoning rather than knowledge tied to a single platform.
